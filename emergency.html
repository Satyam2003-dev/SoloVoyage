Medical emergency

Serious injury, sudden illness or a reaction you cannot manage.

You or someone near you is seriously ill or injured, or symptoms are getting worse quickly.

Immediate priorities

  1. Call the local emergency number, or ask someone nearby to call and stay with you.
  2. Give your exact location first, in case the call drops.
  3. Follow the call-taker's instructions and keep the line open until they tell you to hang up.

What to do next

  • Tell responders about allergies, medical conditions and medicines you take.
  • Call your insurer's 24/7 assistance line as soon as it is practical. They can direct you to a suitable hospital.
  • Ask for a written report and itemized receipts for your claim.
  • Tell your trusted contact where you are being treated.

Avoid

  • Do not move a seriously injured person unless they are in danger where they are.
  • Do not travel to hospital alone if you may lose consciousness.
  • Do not assume treatment is free or that your home insurance applies.

Country differences: Emergency numbers, ambulance coverage and payment rules differ by country. This page is not medical advice.

Theft or robbery

Something was taken from you, with or without a threat.

Your belongings were stolen, or someone is demanding them now.

Immediate priorities

  1. If you are being threatened, hand over what they ask for. Your safety comes first.
  2. Get to a busy, staffed place.
  3. If you are hurt or still in danger, call the local emergency number.

What to do next

  • Block cards through your bank's app or its international lost-card line.
  • Report the theft to the police and ask for a written report or reference number. Insurers usually need one.
  • If your passport was taken, follow the lost passport steps.
  • Change passwords for accounts that were open on a stolen device.
  • Note the time, place and a description while you remember it.

Avoid

  • Do not chase or confront the person.
  • Do not arrange to buy your belongings back yourself.
  • Do not delay the police report. Some insurers set a short deadline.

Country differences: Police reporting differs by country. You may need an interpreter or your embassy's help to find the right station.

Lost or stolen passport

Your passport is missing and you are outside your home country.

You cannot find your passport, or it was stolen.

Immediate priorities

  1. Retrace your steps and check with your hotel, transport operator and any lost property office.
  2. Contact your nearest embassy or consulate and ask about an emergency travel document.
  3. If it was stolen, report it to the local police and get a copy of the report.

What to do next

  • Report the passport lost or stolen to your own passport authority, so it cannot be misused.
  • Gather what they usually ask for: a photocopy, proof of identity, passport photos, the police report and your travel plans.
  • Ask whether the destination requires a replacement visa or entry stamp.
  • Tell your airline that you will travel on a different document.

Avoid

  • Do not use a passport you have reported lost, even if you find it later. Ask the issuing authority first.
  • Do not pay unofficial agents who offer fast replacements.
  • Do not assume every country accepts an emergency travel document. Check your route.

Country differences: The process, cost and time depend on your nationality and where you are. Only your own government can issue your document.

Lost or stolen phone

You have lost your map, tickets and contacts in one go.

Your phone is missing and you may have no other way to navigate or pay.

Immediate priorities

  1. Get somewhere safe with staff and ask to borrow a phone or computer.
  2. Call your number, then use your device finder to locate, lock or erase the phone.
  3. Use your paper backup for your hotel address and key numbers.

What to do next

  • Contact your mobile provider to suspend the SIM.
  • Change the password of your main email account first, then banking and social accounts.
  • Tell your bank and remove cards from mobile wallets.
  • Report a theft to the police if your insurer needs it. Have the device's serial or IMEI number if you saved it.
  • Tell your trusted contact how to reach you.

Avoid

  • Do not go alone to a location shown by a tracker. Give it to the police.
  • Do not follow messages claiming that your phone has been found if they ask you to sign in.
  • Do not stay signed in on a borrowed or public device.

Country differences: Find and lock features work only if you enabled them beforehand and the phone can be reached.

Accommodation emergency

Fire, an intruder, an unsafe room or a booking that does not exist.

Your accommodation is unsafe, or you have nowhere to stay tonight.

Immediate priorities

  1. In a fire, leave by the stairs and call the emergency services once you are outside.
  2. If someone is trying to get in, lock the door, call the front desk and then the emergency number.
  3. If you feel unsafe, move to the lobby or another staffed public place.

What to do next

  • Ask the property for a different room, or leave if it cannot be made safe.
  • Contact the booking platform while you are still there and keep photos and messages.
  • If the booking does not exist, go to a well-reviewed hotel with a 24-hour desk for one night.
  • Update your trusted contact with your new address.

Avoid

  • Do not use an elevator in a fire or go back for belongings.
  • Do not open the door to someone you have not verified.
  • Do not accept a stranger's offer of a place to stay.

Country differences: Building safety rules vary. Check exits and locks yourself when you arrive.

Transport emergency

An accident, a breakdown, an unsafe ride or being stranded.

You have been in an accident, feel unsafe in a vehicle, or cannot get to where you need to be.

Immediate priorities

  1. If anyone is hurt, call the local emergency number.
  2. Move away from traffic if you can do so safely.
  3. If a ride feels wrong, ask to stop in a busy place, get out and call someone.

What to do next

  • Use the emergency feature in your ride-hailing app if it has one, and share your trip live.
  • Photograph the scene, plates and documents when it is safe.
  • Ask for a police report after an accident. Insurers often require it.
  • If you are stranded, wait in a staffed, lit place and book a new ride through an official service.
  • Contact the airline or operator about rebooking before you buy a new ticket.

Avoid

  • Do not accept a lift from a stranger because you are stranded.
  • Do not sign a statement you cannot read.
  • Do not argue with a driver inside the vehicle.

Country differences: Traffic law and passenger rights differ by country and operator.

Natural disaster

Earthquake, storm, flood, wildfire or another major event.

A disaster is happening or an official warning has been issued where you are.

Immediate priorities

  1. Follow instructions from local authorities and leave early if told to evacuate.
  2. Protect yourself from the immediate hazard before anything else.
  3. Take your passport, medicine, phone, charger, water and cash if you can do so without delay.

What to do next

  • Monitor official local alerts, and ask hotel staff to translate.
  • Send a short message to your trusted contact. Texts may get through when calls do not.
  • Register or update your details with your embassy if it offers this.
  • Contact your airline and insurer once you are safe.

Avoid

  • Do not walk or drive through floodwater.
  • Do not return to a damaged building until authorities say it is safe.
  • Do not rely on unverified social media reports.

Country differences: Protective actions depend on the hazard and local guidance. Learn the plan for your destination when you arrive.

Personal security

Being followed, harassed, threatened or assaulted.

Someone is following, harassing or threatening you, or has hurt you.

Immediate priorities

  1. Move toward people, light and staff: a hotel lobby, busy shop or station office.
  2. Call the local emergency number if you are in danger.
  3. Make noise and draw attention if you need help now.

What to do next

  • Tell staff plainly: "I am being followed. Please call the police."
  • Share your live location with your trusted contact and stay on the line.
  • If you have been assaulted, get to safety and seek medical care. You can ask your embassy about local support.
  • Write down or record what you remember when you are ready.

Avoid

  • Do not go straight back to your room if you are being followed.
  • Do not confront the person.
  • Do not blame yourself. What happened is not your fault.

Country differences: Laws and support services for victims vary widely. Your embassy can explain local procedures.

Communication problems

No signal, no battery or no shared language.

You cannot reach anyone, or cannot make yourself understood.

Immediate priorities

  1. Go to a place with staff and Wi-Fi: a hotel, station, cafe or airport desk.
  2. In an emergency, ask someone nearby to call for you.
  3. Use your paper backup for addresses and phone numbers.

What to do next

  • Switch on power saving and charge from a power bank.
  • Try an emergency call even without your own network's signal. Phones can often use any available network.
  • Use an offline translation app, written phrases, gestures or a map.
  • Send your trusted contact a short update when you reconnect, so they do not raise an alarm.

Avoid

  • Do not hand your unlocked phone to a stranger.
  • Do not sign in to sensitive accounts on unknown networks.
  • Do not wander looking for signal in an unfamiliar area at night.

Country differences: Emergency calling without a SIM or roaming plan is not available in every country.

Financial emergency

Cards blocked, cash gone or a scam in progress.

You have no working way to pay, or you think you have been scammed.

Immediate priorities

  1. Freeze your cards in your bank's app, or call its international number.
  2. Use your backup card or emergency cash.
  3. If you are being pressured to pay, stop and move to a public place.

What to do next

  • Ask your bank about emergency cash or a replacement card.
  • Ask family to send money through a reputable transfer service.
  • Contact your embassy if you are destitute. They may help you contact family.
  • Report scams to the police and your bank, and keep receipts and messages.

Avoid

  • Do not share card numbers, PINs or one-time codes with anyone who contacts you.
  • Do not withdraw cash under pressure or at an unfamiliar machine.
  • Do not borrow from strangers.

Country differences: Embassies generally do not lend money or pay bills. Bank services abroad depend on your provider.
